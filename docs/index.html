01 · Question

A wrong answer costs more than asking for help

A support bot that confidently answers the wrong question is worse than one that says "let me connect you to someone". So accuracy alone is the wrong target. The useful questions are which messages the model gets wrong, why, and whether its confidence warns us in advance.

The hypotheses, their pass criteria, the split rules and every constant were committed in PREREGISTRATION.md before any model was trained.

  • H1Most errors come from overlapping intent definitions, not from a weak model.Supported
  • H2Handing off the least confident messages beats handing off random ones, at every coverage level.Supported

  • H4At most 300 SVD dimensions keep at least 90% of the full TF-IDF macro-F1.Supported

02 · Data

13,083 short messages, 77 intents

BANKING77 (Casanueva et al., 2020) contains customer messages to a bank, each labelled with one intent such as card_arrival or exchange_rate. There are 10,003 training messages, with 35 to 187 per intent, and 3,080 test messages, 40 per intent. The files are downloaded at a pinned commit and checked with SHA-256.

  • 4 exact duplicates are removed, leaving 9,999 training messages.
  • 153 near-duplicate pairs (TF-IDF cosine ≥ 0.90) are grouped so a pair never straddles two folds. Otherwise the model would effectively see a held-out message during training, and cross-validation would look better than it is.
  • 10 intents are held out at random for the unseen-intent test.

A first hint about the labels

Some near-identical messages carry different labels. No model can get both of these right:

"has my top up gone through?"pending_top_up
"has my top-up gone through?"top_up_reverted
"how can i get new card?"contactless_not_working
"can i get a new card?"order_physical_card

03 · Preprocessing and methods

Simple features, several model families

Text is lowercased and whitespace is collapsed. Two TF-IDF views are concatenated: word 1–2 grams and character 3–5 grams inside word boundaries, which tolerate typos like "recieved". Counts are damped with 1 + log(count), and each view is L2-normalised. Every learned step (vocabulary, IDF, SVD, scaling) sits inside an sklearn Pipeline, so it is refit on the training folds only.

Hyperparameters follow the one-standard-error rule: take the simplest setting whose cross-validated score is within one standard error of the best. For the main model that gave word + character features with C = 10.

04 · Evaluation design

Decide on cross-validation, confirm once on the test set

  • Pre-registered. Two later changes were added as dated amendments, each before the numbers it affects were computed.
  • 5-fold StratifiedGroupKFold, stratified by intent and grouped by near-duplicate cluster. Every tuning choice, threshold and hypothesis decision uses out-of-fold predictions.
  • The test set was opened once. One script may read it, a test enforces that, and a lock file blocks a second run. It ran on commit c21163f after a full dry run on a training fold.
  • Macro-F1 is the main metric, so all 77 intents count equally.
  • 95% bootstrap intervals (1,000 resamples) on every headline number, paired for model comparisons.
  • Calibration is measured with expected calibration error, 15 bins.
  • Handoff is measured with risk–coverage curves and an explicit cost: a wrong answer costs k, a handoff costs 1.
  • Robustness: five fold seeds (SD 0.002) and a stricter near-duplicate grouping (−0.0008).

05 · Results

A linear model on TF-IDF reaches 0.913

Linear models win on this text. KNN and the random forest trail by six to seven points, and the keyword rule by almost thirty. The ranking is the same in cross-validation and on the test set.

06 · H2 Supported

Confidence knows which answers to hand off

The rule is simple: answer when the top probability reaches a threshold, otherwise hand off. Move the threshold and watch what happens to every hundred messages. The comparison is handing off the same number of messages at random, which removes right and wrong answers alike.

At every pre-registered coverage level from 50% to 95%, the interval for (confidence error − random error) lies below zero, in cross-validation and on the test set. The thresholds were frozen from cross-validation, and on the test set each lands within 0.01 of the best cost achievable there.

Can a second model do better?

E1 learns to predict errors from the main model's own confidence signals. It helps a little, and reliably, but most of the signal is already in the top probability.

07 · H3 Supported

The most accurate model is not the best calibrated

A model is calibrated if, among messages where it says 80%, about 80% are right. Pick a model to see its reliability curve. Points above the diagonal mean the model is less confident than it should be.

The main model is right 90% of the time but says 79% on average. With 77 classes, an L2 penalty keeps the scores small, so probability spreads across similar intents. That is harmless for ranking, which is why the handoff rule still works, but the raw probability should not be read as a literal chance. The random forest is the extreme case: its probability is a vote share spread over many intents.

08 · H4 Supported

A hundred dimensions hold most of the signal

Truncated SVD compresses about 37,000 TF-IDF features into d directions, fit on the training folds only. Logistic regression is then trained on those directions.

Variance is not usefulness. A hundred directions explain only a third of the TF-IDF variance yet keep 94% of the macro-F1, because much of the variance is rare words that say little about intent. The full model is still best, so SVD is a compression here, not an improvement.

09 · Errors · H1 Supported

Most errors come from overlapping intents

Link two intents if the model confuses them at least five times, and call each connected cluster a confusable group. A weak model would scatter its errors. This one concentrates them inside the groups, and merging the groups removes most of them.

A caveat, stated openly: at the pre-registered threshold one group chains 29 intents through shared neighbours, and the share of errors inside groups falls below half at stricter thresholds. The comparison with chance holds at every threshold.

Fifty errors, labelled by hand

The author read 50 randomly sampled errors and assigned each a cause.

Different models, same mistakes

If the labels are the problem, unrelated model families should trip on the same pairs. These are logistic regression's ten most confused pairs, and whether each is also in the top ten of KNN and of the random forest.

10 · Unseen intents Exploratory

Confidence catches most intents it has never seen

Real customers ask things no intent covers. To simulate that, the main model was retrained on 67 intents and shown messages from the 10 it never saw. It has to pick one of the intents it knows, so the only defence is low confidence. The Unseen intents tab in the handoff figure shows both confidence distributions.

More data would still help

12 · Limitations

What these results cannot claim

  • One dataset, one bank, English only. The results may not carry over to other domains or languages.
  • A balanced test set, 40 messages per intent, unlike real traffic where a few intents dominate.
  • One intent per message. Messages with two requests are forced into one label.
  • The cost ratio is assumed, not measured, which is why the handoff figure lets you change it.
  • The 50 error labels and the coarse categories are the author's own judgement, and 50 is a small sample: the 95% interval for "ambiguous label" is roughly 52% to 78%.
  • The H1 grouping chains into one large group at the pre-registered threshold.
  • The unseen-intent test covers only the 10 intents drawn, and is exploratory.
  • Narrowed from the proposal: a small neural network and gradient boosting were dropped to meet the deadline, and the random forest uses one fixed configuration.
  • Course methods only. Pretrained sentence encoders would likely score higher.

13 · Conclusion

Fix the labels first, then let confidence decide

A well-tuned linear model on TF-IDF reaches 0.913 macro-F1 on BANKING77, far above a keyword bot. What remains is mostly not a weak model: the errors concentrate in groups of intents whose definitions overlap, and merging those groups lifts accuracy from 91% to 96.5%.

The model's confidence is too low in absolute terms, yet it ranks its own mistakes well. A threshold chosen in cross-validation answered 85% of test messages at 97% accuracy, and handed off most messages from intents the model had never seen.
